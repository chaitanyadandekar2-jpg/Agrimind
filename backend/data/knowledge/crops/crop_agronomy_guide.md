# Crop Agronomy and Soil Suitability Guide

## Tomato (Solanum lycopersicum)
Tomato is a high-value vegetable crop that thrives best in well-drained, fertile sandy-loam or loam soils.
- Preferred soil pH: 6.0 to 6.8. In highly acidic soils (pH < 5.5), blossom-end rot and phosphorus fixation increase.
- Nutrient needs: Balanced Nitrogen (120-200 kg/ha), Phosphorus (15-30 kg/ha), and generous Potassium (150-300 kg/ha). Potassium is critical for fruit firmness, uniform color development, and lycopene synthesis.
- Salinity sensitivity: Highly sensitive to electrical conductivity (EC > 1.5 dS/m). High salinity stunts growth, induces flower drop, and reduces marketable yield.
- Season: Rabi (winter) and Zaid (summer/spring).
- Chloride sensitivity: Avoid heavy applications of Muriate of Potash (MOP); use Sulphate of Potash (SOP) instead to prevent leaf-tip burn and fruit quality deterioration.

Q: Which fertilizer is suitable for Tomato?
A: For tomato, basal application of DAP or SSP along with Sulphate of Potash (SOP) provides strong root initiation. Nitrogen should be applied in split doses (at planting, 30 days after transplanting, and during flowering/fruit development). If soil tests indicate zinc deficiency, a foliar spray of 0.5% ZnSO4 during early flowering enhances fruit set.

## Wheat (Triticum aestivum)
Wheat is the primary Rabi staple cereal crop grown across northern and central India in loam and clay-loam soils.
- Preferred soil pH: 6.0 to 7.5.
- Nutrient needs: Available N 100-180 kg/ha, P 12-25 kg/ha, K 100-200 kg/ha.
- Salinity sensitivity: Moderately tolerant to salinity up to EC 2.5 dS/m.
- Season: Rabi. Sowing usually occurs between November and December.
- Split application: Nitrogen is most efficient when split: 50% basal at sowing, 25% at crown root initiation (CRI, 21 DAS), and 25% at first flowering or boot stage.

Q: Which crop should I grow in Rabi season if my soil pH is 6.5 to 7.5?
A: Wheat is an ideal Rabi cereal for neutral to slightly alkaline loam soils (pH 6.0-7.5). If available nitrogen is low, plan a three-way split of urea. If soil organic carbon is low, combine with 5 tonnes/ha FYM or vermicompost at land preparation.

## Rice / Paddy (Oryza sativa)
Rice is predominantly a Kharif season wetland cereal requiring heavy irrigation or reliable monsoon rainfall.
- Preferred soil pH: 5.5 to 7.0. It tolerates slightly acidic soil better than other cereals due to submerged reduction chemistry.
- Water requirement: High; needs standing water (submergence) during tillering and panicle development.
- Salinity sensitivity: Moderate tolerance up to EC 2.0 dS/m.
- Micronutrient watch: Submerged soils frequently show zinc deficiency (Khaira disease). Basal application of 25 kg/ha ZnSO4 is strongly recommended in endemic zones.

Q: Why does paddy need zinc application?
A: In submerged wetland paddy soils, chemical reduction immobilizes native zinc, causing characteristic rusty-brown leaf lesions known as Khaira disease. Applying 20-25 kg/ha zinc sulphate basal or spraying 0.5% ZnSO4 corrects this deficiency.

## Cotton (Gossypium hirsutum / arboreum)
Cotton is a deep-rooted Kharif cash crop that performs exceptionally well in deep black cotton soils (Vertisols) and well-drained alluvial soils.
- Preferred soil pH: 6.0 to 8.0. Highly tolerant of slightly alkaline soils.
- Salinity sensitivity: High tolerance to moderate salinity (EC up to 3.0 dS/m).
- Water requirement: Medium; deep taproot provides drought resistance, but waterlogging must be avoided.
- Nutrient needs: N 100-180 kg/ha, P 15-25 kg/ha, K 100-200 kg/ha. Magnesium and boron foliar sprays during square formation prevent leaf reddening and boll shedding.

Q: Is cotton suitable for saline or high-pH soils?
A: Yes, cotton is one of the most salt-tolerant commercial field crops, tolerating soil EC levels up to 3.0 dS/m and pH up to 8.0 in black clay soils, provided drainage is adequate to prevent waterlogging.

## Soybean (Glycine max)
Soybean is an important Kharif legume and oilseed crop.
- Preferred soil pH: 6.0 to 7.5.
- Biological Nitrogen Fixation: As a legume, soybean enters a symbiotic relationship with Rhizobium japonicum bacteria, fixing atmospheric nitrogen. Excessive chemical nitrogen fertilizer suppresses nodulation and causes excessive vegetative growth with poor pod set.
- Nutrient needs: N 60-120 kg/ha, P 15-25 kg/ha, K 100-180 kg/ha. Highly responsive to basal phosphorus (SSP) and sulphur.

Q: How much nitrogen fertilizer does soybean need?
A: Soybean needs only a modest starter dose of nitrogen (20-30 kg N/ha as basal) to establish seedlings. Inoculating seed with Rhizobium culture allows the crop to fix the rest of its nitrogen naturally. Applying excessive urea wastes money and reduces nodule efficiency.

## Onion (Allium cepa)
Onion is a shallow-rooted vegetable crop with high market value.
- Preferred soil pH: 6.0 to 7.0.
- Organic carbon demand: Thrives in high organic-matter soils (OC > 0.6%) which provide loose soil tilth for bulb expansion.
- Salinity sensitivity: Highly sensitive to salinity (EC > 1.2 dS/m). Saline soil causes tip burn and stunted bulb size.
- Chloride sensitivity: Avoid Muriate of Potash (MOP); use Sulphate of Potash (SOP) to enhance shelf life, pungency, and bulb skin quality.

Q: Why is organic carbon crucial for onion cultivation?
A: Onion roots are shallow and non-fibrous. High soil organic carbon (above 0.6%) improves soil aeration, lowers bulk density, retains moisture evenly, and allows smooth bulb expansion without compaction deformities.

## Chickpea / Gram (Cicer arietinum)
Chickpea is a premier Rabi pulse crop grown in rainfed and semi-irrigated regions.
- Preferred soil pH: 6.0 to 7.5. Sensitive to waterlogging and soil salinity (EC > 1.5 dS/m).
- Water requirement: Low; deep taproot makes it ideal for residual moisture conditions.
- Nutrient needs: N 40-80 kg/ha, P 15-25 kg/ha, K 60-120 kg/ha. Responds strongly to phosphorus and sulphur.

Q: Which fertilizer is best for chickpea / gram?
A: Single Super Phosphate (SSP) is the best fertilizer for chickpea because it supplies both phosphorus (16% P2O5) and sulphur (11% S), both of which are critical for root nodulation and pulse protein synthesis. Chemical nitrogen should be kept to a small starter dose.

## Mustard (Brassica juncea)
Mustard is a vital Rabi oilseed crop.
- Preferred soil pH: 6.0 to 7.5.
- Nutrient needs: N 80-140 kg/ha, P 15-25 kg/ha, K 40-80 kg/ha.
- Sulphur requirement: Very high; sulphur is an essential constituent of glucosinolates and oil synthesis. Applying 20-25 kg/ha elemental sulphur or gypsum increases seed oil percentage by 2-4%.

## Groundnut (Arachis hypogaea)
Groundnut is an oilseed legume cultivated in light sandy-loam soils.
- Preferred soil pH: 6.0 to 7.2. Loose sandy loam is necessary for easy peg penetration and pod development.
- Calcium and Sulphur: Calcium is vital during pegging for shell formation (preventing hollow pods or "pops"). Applying agricultural gypsum at 200-400 kg/ha at pegging stage is recommended.
